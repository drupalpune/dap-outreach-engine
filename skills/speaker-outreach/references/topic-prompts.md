# Topic prompts

"Would you like to speak?" gets a maybe. "Would you talk about the thing you
were complaining about in March?" gets a yes. Most people don't think they have
a talk; they have five and haven't labelled them.

When someone is interested but topic-less, offer two or three concrete options
drawn from what you know about them — `expertise_tags` and `notes` are there for
exactly this.

## Prompts that work

- The hardest bug you shipped a fix for this year
- A migration or upgrade you ran, including what went wrong
- A tool or module you maintain, and why you started it
- Something you were sure about two years ago and now aren't
- A production incident and the postmortem
- How your team does a thing everyone does differently: reviews, deploys, estimates
- A conference or DrupalCon recap, for people who couldn't go
- Comparing two approaches you've genuinely used in anger

## Prompts to avoid

- "Intro to <technology>" — the room is working developers, not beginners
- Anything the speaker hasn't personally run in production
- A product walkthrough with no transferable lesson
- A topic they don't actually care about but think the audience wants

## Reading the tags

| `expertise_tags` | Nudge toward |
|---|---|
| `drupal`, `migrations` | Upgrade war stories, D7 end-of-life, content modelling |
| `devops`, `ci-cd` | Deploy pipelines, hosting costs, local dev environments |
| `ai-tooling` | LLMs in editorial workflows, code review, what didn't work |
| `php` | Language-level topics that pull in the wider PHP crowd |
| `frontend` | Theming, decoupled front ends, design systems |
| `security` | Update discipline, audits, incident response |

## Slot pressure

When slots are filling, say so. "We have the lightning slot left and one main
session" is a real constraint people respond to. Only ever say it when it's true —
manufactured scarcity in a community of two hundred people is noticed immediately.
