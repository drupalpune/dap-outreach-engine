# Session formats

Three slots per meetup. Every invite names all three, because the smallest slot
is what converts a first-time speaker.

## Main session — 45 min + 15 min Q&A

The anchor. A worked-through topic with enough depth that people take notes.
Slides expected. Live demos are welcome and should have a screenshot fallback.

Good for: a migration you actually ran, a performance problem you actually fixed,
a module or tool you maintain, an architecture decision and what it cost.

One per meetup. Fill it first — the announcement post needs a headline.

## Lightning talk — 25 min + 5 min Q&A

Long enough to be useful, short enough that preparing it doesn't eat a weekend.
Slides optional. The natural slot for a first-time speaker or a narrow topic.

Good for: one module, one gotcha, one script that saved you a day, a conference
recap, a five-minute demo padded with the story behind it.

One per meetup, occasionally two if the main session runs short.

## BoF (birds of a feather) — 45 min, open discussion

Someone proposes a topic and steers the conversation. **No slides, no
preparation, no talk.** The proposer opens with two minutes of framing and then
mostly asks questions.

Good for: "how is everyone handling Drupal 11 upgrades", "hiring PHP developers
in Pune", "AI tooling in editorial workflows — what actually works", "how do you
price a fixed-bid CMS project".

One per meetup, at the end, when the room is warm.

**Lead with this format for anyone hesitant.** "No slides needed" removes the
single biggest reason people say no. Many BoF proposers come back next quarter
with a main session.

## Choosing a slot for someone

| Signal | Offer |
|---|---|
| Spoken at a conference or with us before | Main session |
| First time speaking anywhere | Lightning, mention BoF as the lighter option |
| Deep expertise, no time to prepare | BoF |
| Strong opinion, wants an argument | BoF |
| `preferred_format` is set | That one, always |
| Travelling into Pune for it | Main session — worth their trip |

## What we tell every speaker

- Talks are recorded only if the speaker agrees. Ask; never assume.
- Slides are the speaker's own; we ask for a PDF afterwards to share with attendees.
- No vendor pitches. Talking about a tool you built or sell is fine as long as
  the talk works for someone who never buys it.
- Bring your own laptop; we'll have HDMI and an adapter.
- Come 20 minutes early for the AV check.
