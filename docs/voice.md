# Voice

Every outgoing draft passes the `unslop` check before it reaches the user. The
general rules are in `skills/unslop/SKILL.md`. This file covers what that means
for outreach mail specifically.

The point is not politeness. A venue mail that reads as machine-written gets
deleted, and the person deleting it is someone you will meet at a conference.

## The mail-specific tells

Beyond the general unslop list, these are the ones that show up in outreach:

1. **"I hope this email finds you well."** Delete. "Hope you're doing well" is
   fine once, in a first touch, to a stranger. Never in a follow-up.
2. **Explaining what Drupal is to a Drupal agency.** Check `org_type` before the
   first sentence. The `drupal-native` angle exists for this.
3. **A crossover paragraph that fits any of sixty orgs.** The test: swap in a
   different org name. If it still reads fine, rewrite it.
4. **"We would be delighted to partner with you."** No partnership, no
   delighting. One room, one Saturday morning.
5. **Three-item lists everywhere.** The logistics ask is genuinely three items.
   Not everything is.
6. **Bold on every proper noun.** Bold the date and the ask. Nothing else.
7. **A closing that asks for nothing.** End with one clear next step, or with the
   easy out. Not both, and never neither.
8. **Hedging stacked on hedging.** "I was wondering if you might possibly be
   open to potentially considering" is one word: "Would".
9. **Em dashes.** Use a full stop or a comma. Reaching for parentheses instead
   just trades one tell for another.
10. **Praise the sender wrote for themselves.** "We run a fantastic meetup." Give
    the number instead. The track-record line from `meetups.csv` does this work.

## The read-aloud test

Read the draft out loud. Anywhere you would not say that sentence to the person
in a corridor, rewrite it. This catches more than any checklist.

## What to keep

Unslop strips AI tells. It should not strip the person. Keep:

- The specific detail that proves you did the reading
- The blunt sentence. "If the timing is wrong, tell me and I'll go away."
- Contractions. "We're", "you'd", "it's".
- Your own turns of phrase, even the slightly odd ones

Sterile is as obvious a tell as flowery. A mail with no voice in it reads as
generated too.

## When it applies

| Output | Unslop? |
|---|---|
| Venue and speaker mails | Yes, always, before showing the user |
| Announcement and recap copy | Yes |
| Fit notes from `prospect-research` | Yes, they end up in mails |
| Notes in the CSV | No, terse is correct there |
| Report output from the scripts | No |
